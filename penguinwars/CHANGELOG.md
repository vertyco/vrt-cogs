# Penguin Wars Changelog

## 0.1.0

Needs ActivityHub 0.8.0 or later.

- First version. Up to six penguins slide into each other on an ice floe that sinks from the edges in, and the last one on the ice wins. CPU penguins fill empty seats.
- Alone, Play starts a round at once and the end card waits for Play again. Two or more people get a 10-second countdown, and the end card says when the next round starts.
- A friend who taps Play during the 3-2-1 takes over a CPU's penguin and plays that round.
- The 3-2-1 beeps, the round starts with "Go!", and the winner hops and flaps in a burst of stars.
- Drag back and let go to slide, tap to brace, grab a fish for one extra-heavy slide, and come back as a seal when you're knocked in.
- Watchers join a line, and after each round the line takes the spots of whoever went out first.
- A dropped player has 20 seconds to come back before a CPU takes over, while someone else is still on the ice. When the last person on the ice leaves, the round stops and nothing is saved.
- A new map every round: one of six ice shapes (the whole floe, a donut, twin islands, a crescent, an hourglass or one full of holes), turned at random. More than half the rounds also get a hazard: gusts of wind, a walrus that slams the ice, or thin ice that cracks under the first penguin on it. The 3-2-1 says which.
- Watchers throw snowballs: a press on the snowball button, then each tap on the ice throws one for 2 of the bot's currency. It nudges the penguins it lands near.
- Every knockout is called out to everyone, the knocked-out player sees who did it, and the last knockout of a round plays in slow motion.
- Nemesis: the person who has knocked you out most (3 times or more) gets a mark on their name that only you see. The call-outs say when they get you again and when you get revenge.
- Profile cards: tap a player's name, a leaderboard row or a watcher's face to see their penguin, play counts, server rank, achievements and nemesis.
- Every penguin starts in a free scarf, which can be swapped for other neck wear or taken off. A faint ring on the ice under each penguin shows its player's color.
- 73 looks in ten kinds: skins, hats, face, neck and back wear, slide trails, a cheer for when you win, a splash for the penguins you knock in, a title under your name and a style for your name. Half are bought with the bot's currency, and half are earned by playing, such as winning 15 rounds or eating 10 fish. Rounds alone against CPUs count toward earning them.
- A wardrobe with a penguin you can turn, a tab for each kind, and Buy, Wear or Take off for whatever you tap. An earned look shows what it takes and how far along you are, and the end card says when a round unlocked one.
- Crate drops: anyone in the window pays to drop a crate on the ice mid-round. The first penguin to reach it gets a shield, a frost that freezes everyone else, or three extra-heavy slides. The buyer can't place it.
- Party rounds: pay to make the next round a fish frenzy, slippery ice, turbo or fast melt for everyone.
- Server admins set the prices, or the bot owner with a global bank. Nothing worn changes how a penguin plays, and the game never pays currency out.
- The wardrobe opens from the cards, or from a Looks button while watching a round. Opening it gives up your seat and your place in the line.
- A top 10 for each server, by wins and then knockouts, for rounds with at least two people.
- Sliders for the bot owner, which change the game's numbers while it runs.
- The sound button opens a small panel with an on/off switch and a volume slider for the sound effects, saved on each device. The button's speaker is crossed out while they are off.
- A slide or brace let go a moment before the wait is over still goes, as soon as it can.
- The ring under your penguin swells as your next move comes ready, and your seal's name dims while it can't dive yet.
- Buttons give under a press and tick, each number of the 3-2-1 pops, and a win shows a trophy.
- With no round on, the page draws 30 times a second, so a phone waiting in the lobby stays cool.
- Works on phones held upright or sideways, and on desktop.
