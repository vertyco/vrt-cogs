# Tanks Changelog

## 0.3.0

- All-new art and sound. Tanks is now a remake: the rules, weapons, prices and every number stay the same, and none of the Flash game's pictures or sounds remain.
- New tanks, barrels, shells, shields, parachutes, trees and 26 shop icons, all drawn in one cartoon style.
- Three new landscapes (snowy peaks, green hills and a desert of red rock), with a line along the top of the ground.
- New explosions with a shock ring, and a new teleport light column, tank wreck and turn marker. Each lasts exactly as long as before, and every blast still covers the crater it digs.
- New sound effects from Kenney's free packs, each played a little higher or lower and louder or quieter every time.
- New menu icon and preview picture.

## 0.2.1

- On a phone held upright, the shop, setup and results screens scroll again. A long shop ran off the bottom of the screen with no way to reach the items below or the Done button.

## 0.2.0

Needs ActivityHub 0.5.0 or later.

- Each tank has its name floating over it, so everyone can tell whose tank is whose. On a phone the names are drawn bigger so they can be read, cut shorter, and names that would touch stack above each other, with a thin line down to the tank of each one that moved up.
- "2 watching" is now a row of the watchers' pictures. Hover one (or tap it on a phone) to see who it is.
- The hub's people button sits in the header, and the menu and people panel show what you're doing: "Aiming · round 2 of 5", "Shopping · round 2 of 5", "Finished 1st" or "Watching".
- Join from the hub's people list asks first while you're in a match.

## 0.1.1

- Tanks start each round with 250 fuel, as in the original, instead of 500. That is 125 pixels of driving for the whole round, so a tank can no longer drive right up to an enemy and fire point blank.

## 0.1.0

- First release: turn-based artillery for ActivityHub, ported from 2DPlay's Flash game Tanks. Up to five players and computer tanks, all 14 weapons and 12 shop items, three landscapes, a time limit for turns and the shop that the host sets (1 minute by default, or no limit; none for one person against computers), and a global leaderboard ranked by match wins.
